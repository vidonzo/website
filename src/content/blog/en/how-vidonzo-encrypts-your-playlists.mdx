Your Xtream credentials are, in effect, your paid subscription. Anyone holding
them can watch on your account, get you rate-limited, or get you cut off. That
makes them the single most sensitive thing Vidonzo touches — and the thing we
least want to be able to read.

So we made it impossible to.

## The split

Not everything needs the same treatment. Syncing favourites across devices is
useful and unremarkable; syncing a provider password is neither. We split the
data accordingly:

**End-to-end encrypted**: your Xtream host, username and password, and any M3U
source URL.

**Stored in plain text, protected by per-user access rules**: favourites, watch
progress, app settings, and the label you gave a playlist — which is your own
words, not a secret.

That split is deliberate. Encrypting favourites would buy almost nothing and
would stop a newly added device from showing anything useful until it is
unlocked.

## How the encryption works

A random 256-bit **data key** is generated on your device — never on a server.
Each source record is encrypted with AES‑256‑GCM under that key, with a unique
nonce per record.

The data key itself is then wrapped: your sync password goes through **Argon2id**
to derive a key-encryption key, and the data key is encrypted with it. Only the
wrapped result is uploaded. The sync password never crosses the network.

What our database holds for a playlist is a label, a kind, and a blob of
ciphertext. There is no key on the server to decrypt it with.

## Adding a second device

The interesting problem is not encryption; it is getting the data key onto your
next device without ever sending it in the clear.

1. The new device signs in. Favourites and progress appear immediately — they
   were never encrypted.
2. Playlists show as **locked**.
3. The new device generates an ephemeral X25519 key pair and shows a QR code.
4. A device that is already unlocked scans it and asks you to confirm.
5. The unlocked device derives a shared secret over ECDH, encrypts the data key
   with it, and uploads that sealed blob.
6. The new device decrypts it with its private key and stores the result in the
   iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore.

At no point does the server hold anything it can open.

## The part we cannot soften

**If you lose your sync password and have no unlocked device left, your
playlists are gone.** Not withheld — mathematically unavailable. We cannot reset
it, because there is nothing on our side to reset.

This is the honest trade of real end-to-end encryption, and every product that
claims it either makes this trade or is not doing it. The app says so plainly at
the moment you set the password, rather than burying it in a policy page.

Re-adding a playlist takes a minute. That is the recovery path, and we would
rather offer that than hold a key we could be compelled to use.
