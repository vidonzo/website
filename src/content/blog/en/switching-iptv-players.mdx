People stay on a player they do not like for the same reason they stay on a
bank they do not like: the move feels like it means setting everything up
again. With an IPTV player it does not, and the reason is structural. The
player never owned your library. [Your playlist is yours](/blog/bring-your-own-playlist/),
issued by your provider, and every honest player is just a different set of
eyes on the same list. Here is what actually travels, what does not, and how
to do it in an evening.

## What travels: everything that matters

**The playlist itself.** Whichever shape you have — an Xtream login or an
M3U/M3U8 link — it is a piece of text you were given, and it works in the new
player exactly as it did in the old. If you cannot remember it, do not dig
through the old app; ask your provider for it again, or check the message they
sent when you signed up. Providers commonly send both an Xtream login and an
M3U address; [either is fine](/blog/xtream-codes-vs-m3u/), and the login is
usually the richer of the two.

**The guide address.** If your provider gave you a separate XMLTV URL for the
programme guide, that goes into the new player too, next to the source it
belongs to. If you use an Xtream login, the guide comes with it and there is
nothing to move.

**Your habits.** The channels you actually watch are the same channels. Ten
minutes with the favourites button on the first evening restores the shape of
your list, and it is often a better shape than the one you had — most people
have favourites they added in the first week and never watched again.

## What does not travel, and why it does not matter

**Favourites and resume points from the old app.** These live in the old
app's own storage, in a format of its own, and no player can read another's.
Favourites you rebuild in minutes. Resume points are lost — for the two or
three things you are midway through, note the episode. That is the whole cost
of the move.

**The old app's settings.** Buffer sizes, decoder choices, subtitle styling —
do not try to copy them. They were tuned to a different engine. Start from the
new player's defaults and adjust only what you can see is wrong.

## What you should *not* bring: an exported file

Some players offer to export your setup as a file. It is tempting to think of
that as the migration path. It usually is not: the export contains the same
login you already have, wrapped in a format only the exporting app reads, and
sometimes it also contains that app's cached copy of the playlist — frozen at
the moment of export, so
[it goes stale the way a local file does](/blog/playlist-url-vs-local-file/).
Skip the file. Enter the login or link into the new player, so it fetches the
current list from the source.

## The order that takes fifteen minutes

1. **Write down the login or link.** Server address, username, password —
   or the M3U URL — and the XMLTV address if you have one. Paste them
   somewhere you can copy from; typing a 24-character password by hand is how
   [a link fails to load](/blog/playlist-link-wont-load/) with no explanation.
2. **Install the new player and add the source.** For Vidonzo,
   [the walkthrough is about a minute](/blog/set-up-your-first-playlist/). Do
   not delete the old app yet.
3. **Check three things**: the guide at a time you can verify, a channel on
   the connection you actually have, and a search for something you know is in
   the list. If all three pass, the switch is essentially done.
4. **Set the refresh policy.** A hosted playlist changes; tell the player how
   often to re-read it — daily suits most people, hourly if your provider
   shuffles things a lot — and use *Refresh now* whenever something seems
   missing.
5. **Rebuild favourites** over the first evening, and let continue-watching
   fill itself in.
6. **Remove the old app** a week later, once you have stopped opening it by
   habit. If it held your provider login, delete it from there rather than
   leaving a copy of your subscription on the phone.

## Two providers, or one on two devices

If the reason for the switch is that you now have two providers — or the same
provider on both a login and a link — a player that
[keeps sources side by side](/blog/multiple-playlists-one-app/) means you never
have to choose. And if the switch coincides with a new phone or a television,
sign in and [everything you rebuild follows you](/blog/move-to-a-new-phone/)
to the next screen without doing it twice.

## The one thing that changes

What actually differs after the switch is not the channels — it is what the
player does with them: whether the guide is right, whether a dropped stream
reconnects by itself, whether films and series are a library or a list, and
what the app knows about you. That is why the move is worth making and why it
costs so little. The library was never in the app.
