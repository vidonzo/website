Somewhere in a lot of living rooms is a small black Android box, five or six
years old, with a gigabyte or two of memory and a chip that was cheap when it
was new. It used to play everything. Now the app it runs stutters on channel
change, the picture judders on busy scenes, and the menu takes a beat to
respond. The instinct is that the box is finished. It usually is not. The
hardware in even a modest box has a dedicated video decoder that is perfectly
capable of drawing 1080p all evening; what has changed is how much *other*
work the software around it has taken on. Here is where the cost goes, and how
to give the work back to the parts that can carry it.

## What actually costs on a weak box

Three things, in decreasing order.

**Decoding video in software.** If the app decodes on the general-purpose
CPU instead of the video block, a 1080p stream eats most of a small chip and
a 4K or HEVC stream simply cannot be done. This single choice separates a box
that plays and a box that stutters, and it is a setting.

**Drawing an elaborate interface.** Posters, animations, blurred backgrounds,
a carousel of a hundred cards, all being redrawn while the video plays
underneath. Every frame of that comes out of the same small budget as the
video does. A heavy interface on top of a light decoder is how a box that
*can* play looks as though it cannot.

**Holding a huge playlist in memory the wrong way.** A provider's full list
is a large file, and an app that keeps all of it as a flat structure and
searches it by scrolling will run a small box out of memory, at which point
the operating system starts closing things — sometimes the app itself.

Notice what is not on the list: the network. Buffering and stutter look alike
on a slow box, so it is worth ruling out the network first —
[the buffering article](/blog/fix-iptv-buffering/) covers that in five
minutes — before assuming the hardware.

## The settings that matter

**Hardware decoding on.** In Vidonzo it is *Settings → Playback → Hardware
decoding*, and on an old box it is the whole game. On, the video block does
the decoding and the CPU is left to draw the interface; off, the CPU does
both and loses. If a channel shows [sound but no picture](/blog/sound-but-no-picture/)
with it on, that channel's format is one the box's decoder does not support —
try the toggle the other way for that channel only, and expect it to be
heavier.

**Buffer sized to the connection, not to the box.** A larger buffer costs
memory, and a small box has little to spare — but a buffer that is too small
stalls on the first hiccup and stalling is what you are trying to cure. Start
at the default; only raise it if the picture actually stops, and only lower it
if the box is plainly short of memory (apps closing on their own is the tell).

**Keep the guide, drop the decoration.** The programme guide is data, not
pictures, and cheap to draw. It is the artwork-heavy screens that cost.
Vidonzo's television layout is [a side rail with focus rings](/blog/iptv-player-on-android-tv/),
built for a d-pad and light by design — the box draws a list, not a showroom.

## The habits that matter

**Curate.** [Favourites and a couple of categories](/blog/organize-large-playlists/)
are not just convenient on a small box; they are lighter. Browsing a
short list of things you actually watch costs almost nothing. Scrolling the
whole provider list every evening is the most expensive thing you can ask a
weak device to do.

**Let the phone do the browsing.** This is the change that surprises people
most. If a phone or tablet on the same Wi-Fi [acts as the box's remote](/blog/turn-your-phone-into-a-tv-remote/),
the phone draws the posters, runs the search and handles the typing — the
box only has to receive "play this" and play it. A weak box with a phone
beside it behaves like a much better box, because it has been demoted to the
one job it is good at.

**Restart it occasionally.** Not folk wisdom: a box that has been on for
three weeks has accumulated whatever every app on it leaked. A restart once a
week costs a minute and returns memory the player can use.

**Uninstall what you do not use.** Every app that runs a background service
is spending the same small budget. A box that only has to be a box has more
left for the picture.

## When it really is the hardware

There is a floor. A box that cannot decode HEVC in hardware will never play
HEVC smoothly, whatever the software does, and providers are moving to HEVC
because it halves their bandwidth. If your provider's channels are mostly
HEVC and your box's decoder predates it, the honest answer is a newer device
— and it need not be expensive; a current certified stick decodes HEVC in
hardware and costs little.

But try the software first. Hardware decoding on, buffer at the default, the
interface kept lean, the phone doing the browsing: a majority of "old box"
complaints do not survive that list, and it takes an evening to work through.
[Setting the playlist up on a fresh player](/blog/set-up-your-first-playlist/)
is about a minute of the evening; the rest is watching to see whether it
stutters.
