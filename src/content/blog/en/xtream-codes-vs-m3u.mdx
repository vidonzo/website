When you pay for an IPTV subscription, the provider hands you one of two things:
a set of **Xtream Codes** login details, or an **M3U** link. They can carry the
exact same channels, yet they behave very differently once they reach your
player. Knowing which is which saves you a surprising amount of trouble.

Both are just ways of describing *where your streams live*. Neither is the
content, and neither is the app. A player like Vidonzo reads whichever one you
have and turns it into a channel list you can actually watch.

## What an M3U playlist is

An M3U file is a plain-text list. Open one and you see pairs of lines: a name and
a stream URL, repeated for every channel. The `.m3u8` variant is the same idea
saved as UTF-8, which is why it handles non-Latin channel names cleanly.

Its strength is that it is universal — almost every player understands it. Its
weakness is that it is *flat and frozen*. The list is only as fresh as the moment
you loaded it. When your provider adds channels or reorganises categories, an M3U
does not know until you fetch it again.

## What Xtream Codes is

Xtream Codes is not a file but an **API**. You enter three things — a host, a
username, and a password — and the player asks the server, live, for your live
channels, films, series, and the programme guide, already sorted into categories.

That structure is the advantage. You get real categories, series grouped into
seasons and episodes, account status, and an EPG without any extra setup. The
trade-off is sensitivity: those three credentials *are* your subscription. Anyone
who copies them can watch on your account, so how a player stores them matters.
This is exactly why Vidonzo keeps Xtream credentials
[encrypted so that even we cannot read them](/blog/why-a-player-should-encrypt-your-credentials/).

## Which one to choose

- **Choose Xtream** if your provider offers it and you want categories, series,
  and a programme guide to appear on their own. It is the richer experience, and
  the one most people should prefer.
- **Choose M3U** when that is all your provider gives you, when you are combining
  sources by hand, or when you want the simplest possible list. It just works,
  everywhere.

If your provider gives you both, start with Xtream and keep the M3U link as a
fallback. There is no lock-in either way — a good player treats them as two doors
into the same room.

## The part that does not change

Whichever format you hold, the thing doing the work is the player. It should read
your list without ads, without tracking what you watch, and without holding your
credentials in a form it can read back. Once you have picked a format,
[setting up your first playlist](/blog/set-up-your-first-playlist/) takes about a
minute — and if a stream ever stutters, that is usually
[a network issue with a quick fix](/blog/fix-iptv-buffering/), not the format's
fault.
