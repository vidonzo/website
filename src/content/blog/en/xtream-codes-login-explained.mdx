**An Xtream Codes login is three pieces of information: a server URL (usually
with a port number, such as `http://example.com:8080`), a username and a
password.** Together they let a player ask your provider's server for your live
channels, films, series and programme guide directly, instead of reading a
fixed list. Almost every problem with this kind of login comes from one of the
three being copied slightly wrong, and each mistake has a recognisable symptom.

## Where the three fields come from

Your provider sends them, usually in a message that looks something like this:

```
URL:      http://example.com:8080
Username: jsmith42
Password: k7Qp9xWz
```

Sometimes there is no separate login at all, just one long link. That link
contains all three:

```
http://example.com:8080/get.php?username=jsmith42&password=k7Qp9xWz&type=m3u_plus&output=ts
```

Read it left to right: everything before `/get.php` is the **server URL**, the
value after `username=` is the **username**, and the value after `password=` is
the **password**. The rest only tells the server which playlist format to send.

It works the other way round too. With the three fields you can write that link
yourself — server URL, then `/get.php?username=`, your username, `&password=`,
your password, `&type=m3u_plus` — which is useful for a player that only accepts
M3U links.

## The server URL, piece by piece

`http://example.com:8080` has three parts, and each one matters.

- **The scheme — `http://` or `https://`.** They are not interchangeable. Use
  exactly what your provider wrote. Many Xtream servers only speak plain
  `http`, and asking for `https` gets you a certificate error or nothing at all.
- **The host — `example.com`**, or sometimes a bare number address such as
  `203.0.113.10`. One wrong letter and the server is simply never found.
- **The port — `:8080`.** The number after the colon is a separate door on the
  same server. `http://example.com` and `http://example.com:8080` are different
  addresses, and leaving the port off is the most common reason a login that
  "looks right" cannot reach anything.

What does **not** belong in the field: anything after the port. Paths such as
`/get.php`, `/player_api.php` or `/c/` are what the player adds itself.

Vidonzo tidies the obvious cases for you: it adds `http://` if you leave the

## Username and password

Both are case-sensitive, and both are usually generated strings where `O` and
`0`, or `l` and `I`, look alike. So:

- **Paste rather than type.** Copy from your provider's message.
- **Watch for a trailing space.** Copying from a message often takes the space
  after the word too, and the server sees it as part of the password.
- **Do not swap them.** It sounds obvious; with two random strings it happens.

A login is as sensitive as a bank password: anyone holding it can use your
account, often locking you out when the connection limit is reached. Vidonzo
stores it only on your device, and
[can encrypt it before it goes anywhere](/blog/why-a-player-should-encrypt-your-credentials/).

## What the player does with it

When you connect, the player calls the server's `player_api.php` with your
username and password. The first answer is about the account itself — whether
the login is accepted, and details such as how many connections it allows at
once. After that come the categories, the channel list, the film and series
catalogues, and the programme guide, each fetched on demand.

That is the difference from a plain M3U link, which is one fixed file with
nothing to ask. [Which of the two to use, when you have both](/blog/xtream-codes-vs-m3u/),
depends mainly on whether you want films, series and a guide.

## What the error messages mean

- **"Username or password is incorrect."** The server was reached and refused
  the login. Check the case, the look-alike characters and the trailing space.
  Some servers answer an expired or suspended account the same way, so if you
  are sure of the password, ask your provider about the account's status.
- **"Couldn't reach the server."** Nothing answered at that address. The usual
  suspects are a missing or wrong port, `https` where `http` was meant, or a
  typo in the host. Rarely, the network you are on blocks unusual ports; trying
  the same login on mobile data tells you quickly.
- **"The server took too long to respond."** It was reached but was slow. A very
  large catalogue can take a while on the first connection; try again before
  changing anything.
- **"The server's security certificate couldn't be verified."** You are using
  `https` with a server whose certificate is invalid. Use the scheme your
  provider specified.
