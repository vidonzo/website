The frustrating version of this problem is the one with no error message. The
source saves. The app looks satisfied. And then either the list is empty, or the
list is perfect and every channel you touch does nothing.

Those are not the same fault, and the fastest way to waste an evening is to
treat them as one. Adding a playlist involves four separate steps, each of which
fails in its own recognisable way:

1. **The fetch** — the app asks a server for the playlist.
2. **The parse** — the app reads what came back.
3. **The stream request** — the app asks for one channel's video.
4. **The decode** — the device turns that video into a picture.

Work out which step you are stuck on first. Every fix below only makes sense
once you know.

## Step 1: the fetch — nothing arrives

The signature is an immediate failure when you save the source: a connection
error, a timeout, or a refusal that appears before you have had time to blink.

**The link expired or rotated.** Providers change host names and re-issue links,
sometimes on a schedule and sometimes without warning. A URL that worked last
month and fails today, with no other change on your side, is the ordinary case
rather than the exotic one.

**The host is written slightly wrong.** For Xtream, the host field wants exactly
what your provider gave you — scheme and port included, nothing else. The two
common mistakes are pasting an address copied out of a browser after it has
picked up a path, and adding or removing a trailing slash. `http://` and
`https://` are not interchangeable here either; a server listening on one will
simply not answer on the other.

**The name does not resolve, or the host is unreachable from this network.** Try
the same source on a different connection — mobile data instead of home Wi-Fi is
the quickest test. If it works on one network and not the other, nothing about
your playlist is wrong, and a proxy is the sort of tool that exists for exactly
this.

## Step 2: the parse — something arrives, but it is not a playlist

The signature here is the quiet one: the source saves apparently fine and the
channel list is empty, or almost.

This is usually a server answering with an *error page* rather than a playlist.
An expired subscription, an exceeded limit or a mistyped username often produces
a perfectly valid HTTP response containing HTML that says so. The app fetched
something, so the fetch succeeded; there were no channels in it, so the list is
empty. That combination — successful save, zero channels — points here almost
every time.

The other parse-stage symptom is cosmetic and unmistakable: the channels are all
there, but the names are mangled into question marks and accented rubbish. That
is a text-encoding mismatch, and it is
[the entire difference between M3U and M3U8](/blog/m3u-vs-m3u8/).

## Step 3: the stream request — the list is perfect, nothing starts

This is the category people find hardest, because everything *looks* right. The
list is complete, the categories are populated, the logos loaded. You press a
channel and get a spinner, then nothing — and crucially, **every** channel
behaves identically.

That uniformity is the diagnostic. When all channels fail the same way at the
same speed, the problem is your account rather than any stream.

**Someone else is watching.** Subscriptions carry a limit on simultaneous
connections, often one or two. If another device in the house — or one you
forgot you left playing in another room — holds the connection, your channel is
refused. This is the single most common cause of "the list is there and nothing
plays", and it is invisible because nothing tells you.

**The subscription lapsed.** Some servers keep serving the channel list after
the account expires and refuse only the streams. The list you are looking at may
be a memory.

**The account is locked to somewhere you are not.** Some providers bind access
to an address or region, so a stream that plays at home refuses on a different
connection while the list still loads normally.

**Everything is up but the server is overloaded.** Distinguishable by timing: it
tends to be evenings, it tends to affect popular channels first, and it looks
like [buffering rather than refusal](/blog/fix-iptv-buffering/).

## Step 4: the decode — most channels work, a few do not

If the list plays and specific channels do not, you have left this article's
territory. A fault that hits *some* channels and not others is almost never the
account and almost never the network — it is
[what your device can and cannot decode](/blog/why-some-channels-dont-play/).

## The symptom table

| What you see | The step that failed |
|---|---|
| Error the moment you save the source | Fetch |
| Source saves, list is empty | Parse — usually an error page |
| Channel names are garbled | Parse — text encoding |
| Full list, every channel fails instantly | Stream request — usually a connection limit |
| Full list, everything stalls in the evening | Stream request — server load |
| Most channels play, a few refuse | Decode |

## What to change, and in what order

Change one thing at a time, and re-test after each. In rough order of how often
it is the answer:

1. **Stop playback everywhere else**, then try again. Free the connection before
   you touch anything else.
2. **Re-enter the source by hand** rather than pasting, watching for the trailing
   slash and the scheme.
3. **Test on a different network.** This separates you-and-your-ISP from
   your-provider in one step.
4. **Ask your provider whether the link has changed.** Not a defeat — link
   rotation is routine, and no amount of local troubleshooting will fix it.

## What the player controls, and what it does not

A player cannot invent access it was refused, and it should not pretend
otherwise: the honest behaviour is a clear failure rather than a spinner that
never resolves.

What it can do is make the diagnosis possible. Keeping several sources side by
side lets you test one against another instead of guessing. A tunable cache
lets you trade latency for stability on a line that is merely weak rather than
broken. Proxy support gets you past a network that blocks the host rather than a
provider that refuses you. And automatic recovery means a connection that drops
for three seconds rejoins the stream instead of dropping you back to a list.

None of that is a fix for an expired subscription. It is the difference between
knowing which of the four steps failed and changing settings at random until
something happens.
