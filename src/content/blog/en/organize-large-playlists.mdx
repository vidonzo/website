A typical IPTV subscription arrives with somewhere between two and twenty
thousand channels. Perhaps forty of them are ones you will ever deliberately
choose. The gap between those two numbers is the whole user-experience problem,
and no amount of scrolling solves it.

The fix is not a better scroll. It is to stop browsing the full list at all.

## Favourites: the list you actually watch

Mark forty channels and the other nineteen thousand nine hundred and sixty stop
mattering. This is the single highest-return minute you will spend on your setup,
and most people skip it because the list looks too big to start on.

Do not try to curate it in one sitting. Add a channel the moment you find
yourself hunting for it a second time. After a fortnight the list converges on
your actual habits, which is more accurate than anything you would have planned
in advance.

Two details worth knowing:

- **Favourites live with you, not with the playlist.** Replace an expired
  subscription with a new one from the same provider and your favourites should
  survive, because they are your data rather than the list's.
- **They belong on the device you sit with.** Marking a favourite from the phone
  while the television plays is far quicker than doing it with a d-pad — which is
  [the whole argument for using the phone as the remote](/blog/turn-your-phone-into-a-tv-remote/).

## Categories: what the format gives you

How much structure you get depends on what your provider handed you, and this is
one of the clearest practical differences between the two formats.

**Xtream Codes** delivers real categories from the server: live channels grouped
by genre or country, films, and series properly nested into seasons and episodes.
The grouping is the provider's, delivered live, and it updates when they change
it.

**An M3U playlist** carries at most a `group-title` on each line. Many providers
fill it in usefully. Some fill it in with one group called `LIVE`. You get what
is in the file, frozen at the moment you fetched it — which is
[the core difference between the two](/blog/xtream-codes-vs-m3u/).

If your list arrives as one undifferentiated wall of channels, that is the file
talking. A player can search it and let you favourite from it, but it cannot
invent groupings that were never provided.

## Search: for everything else

Search is what makes the long tail reachable. The channel you want once a month
does not deserve a favourite slot and does not deserve five minutes of scrolling
either.

A few things make search work better in practice:

- **Type less.** Three or four characters is usually enough; longer strings are
  more likely to miss on a spelling you did not expect.
- **Watch for naming conventions.** Providers prefix heavily — `UK: `, `FR|`,
  `[HD] `. Searching for the distinctive part of the name rather than the start
  of it avoids the prefix entirely.
- **Search from the phone.** Text entry with a real keyboard versus an on-screen
  grid is not a close comparison.

## Keeping it tidy over time

Playlists rot. Providers rename channels, retire them, and reshuffle categories,
and a favourites list slowly fills with entries that no longer resolve.

- **When a favourite stops working, check a neighbour first.** One dead entry is
  usually a renamed channel. A whole dead category is a provider change.
- **Refresh rather than re-add.** Re-fetching an M3U or letting the Xtream API
  resync brings in renames without you rebuilding anything.
- **Prune once a season.** Two minutes, and the list stops accumulating debris.
- **Do not confuse a dead favourite with a buffering one.** A channel that starts
  and stutters is [a network path problem](/blog/fix-iptv-buffering/); a channel
  that never starts at all is usually gone from the provider's line-up.

## What organizing does not fix

It is worth being clear about the limit. Favourites, categories and search are
about finding things. They do not change what is in your subscription, they do
not make a stream more reliable, and they do not add channels that were never in
your list —
[the player reads the list you brought, and that is the whole job](/blog/what-an-iptv-player-does/).

What they do change is whether an evening starts with a decision or with a
scroll.
