Every app you install has already settled one question before you open it: who
pays for it. When the answer is an advertiser, that answer does not stay in the
business model. It works its way down into the architecture and surfaces in
places you would never think to look — a background process, a device
identifier, a request to a domain you have never heard of.

Which is why a player with no ads and no trackers does not protect your privacy
by having a feature. It protects it by not containing something that should
never have been in a media player in the first place.

Vidonzo contains no advertising SDK, no analytics library, and no behavioural
tracking. Not a reduced amount. None.

That is easy to claim and easy to check: the app's dependency list is short and
public, and there is no network call to anything that is not your own provider,
your own guide URL, or — if you turn sync on — our sync service.

## Why it matters more here than elsewhere

An IPTV player sees an unusually intimate stream of data. What you watch, when
you watch it, how long you stay, what you rewind. In an ad-funded app that is
not a side effect; it is the product. Bundling an ad SDK into a player means
handing that behavioural stream to a third party you have never heard of, on
every device you install it on.

A record of what a household watches is also not the kind of thing you can ask
for back. What has left has left.

We would rather not have it.

## What the absence actually changes

Three things, and none of them ask you to take our word for anything.

- **There is no third party in the picture.** Where there is no advertising SDK,
  there is no company gathering your viewing habits off your device and setting
  them beside whatever it already holds about you from somewhere else.
- **The network destinations are countable.** Your own provider, your own guide
  URL, and the sync service if you have switched it on. The list is that short,
  and its shortness is the part that can be inspected.
- **Nothing extra runs in the background.** The player adds no overhead of its
  own, because there is no advert cycling behind the video and no tracker
  steadily gathering something to send.

None of that is a posture that could be revised next quarter. It follows from
what the app is: [a player you bring your own playlist
to](/blog/bring-your-own-playlist/), with no catalogue to promote and no
recommendations to train.

## What we lose

Being honest about the trade: we do not know how many people use the app, which
features get touched, or where users give up. When something breaks, we find out
because someone writes to us, not because a dashboard went red.

That is a genuine cost, and it makes the app slower to improve in places. We
think it is the right cost to pay for a category of app that sits this close to
what you watch at home.

## What that means for money

Vidonzo is free. There is no subscription, no paid tier gating playback
features, and the app processes no payments at all. The only money in this
picture is what you already pay your own IPTV provider — which has nothing to do
with us.

## What we do store

If you never sign in, nothing at all leaves your device. If you turn on sync, we
store your email, your encrypted playlist sources, your favourites, watch
progress and settings, and the devices you have linked. That list is exhaustive
and it is written out in full in the [privacy policy](/privacy/).

Not "we may collect certain information to improve your experience". Just the
list.

## The why, in one line

Data that was never collected cannot leak, cannot be sold, and cannot one day
end up with someone you never chose. There is no policy to trust and no promise
to keep, because there is nothing sitting there to be careless with.

That leaves the app with one job — [reading the list you brought and putting it
on the screen](/blog/what-an-iptv-player-does/) — which was the whole of the job
to begin with.
