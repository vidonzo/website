Every discussion of IPTV screens jumps between the two extremes — the
television on the wall and the phone in your pocket — and skips the screen in
between. The tablet deserves better. It is the only screen in the house that is
both big enough to actually watch and light enough to follow you around, and
for live television that combination is worth more than it sounds.

## Where a tablet genuinely wins

**The kitchen.** Propped against the tiles, a tablet is a cooking-hour
television that no one had to mount on a wall. The match plays next to the
chopping board.

**The bedside.** A tablet on the duvet is a private cinema for one; with
headphones in, nobody else's evening is disturbed. For households that share
one living-room TV, this is the second television that never had to be bought.

**The second-screen evening.** During a big fixture, the living-room TV shows
one match; the tablet quietly plays [the guide's](/blog/epg-explained/) other
one beside you on the sofa.

**The guest screen.** Someone staying over gets a full television experience
handed to them, no remote-control diplomacy involved.

## The interface finally fits the device

Here is the quiet advantage: most of what is awkward about IPTV is awkward
because of the remote control.
[On a TV box](/blog/iptv-player-on-android-tv/), entering a URL with a d-pad is
purgatory and search is a chore. On a tablet, all of it becomes ordinary
touchscreen work — [the first-playlist setup](/blog/set-up-your-first-playlist/)
is a minute of typing on a real keyboard, scrolling
[a several-thousand-line channel list](/blog/organize-large-playlists/) is a
flick, and search is just typing.

The screen is also close enough to touch, which changes what an interface can
be. A programme grid designed for a d-pad must be sparse; the same grid at
arm's length can afford density that would be illegible from three metres.

## Same playlist, another screen

A tablet does not need its own arrangement with anyone.
[Your playlist is yours](/blog/bring-your-own-playlist/); adding it to a second
device is the same minute of setup, and
[moving to a new device later](/blog/move-to-a-new-phone/) carries everything
along. One honest caveat: many providers limit how many streams one
subscription may play *simultaneously*, so if the TV and the tablet fight over
the same channel at the same time, that is the limit you are meeting — check
your arrangement rather than the app.

It is worth being deliberate about Wi-Fi, too. A tablet is a wireless device by
definition, and the kitchen or bedroom is often at the fussy edge of coverage —
if the picture freezes in exactly one room,
[the radio link is the suspect](/blog/fix-iptv-freezing-on-wifi/), not the
stream.

## Small screen, close eyes

Two settings earn their keep at arm's length. Subtitles sized for a television
are enormous on a tablet held close;
[a player that lets you set subtitle size and contrast](/blog/subtitles-and-audio-tracks/)
turns them from a banner back into a caption. And headphones are the tablet's
natural companion — late-night sport without a sleeping household hearing the
crowd.

## When the TV is still better

Honesty requires the other column. Shared watching belongs on the biggest
screen; a film with the family is not a tablet occasion. And when you *are* on
the sofa in front of the television, the tablet's best role changes — it
becomes [the remote control the TV never shipped with](/blog/turn-your-phone-into-a-tv-remote/),
searching and steering while the big screen just plays.

The point is not to crown a winner. It is that a playlist you own is not tied
to a piece of furniture. Television used to be a place in the house; with a
player on every screen, it is simply whatever room you happen to be in.
