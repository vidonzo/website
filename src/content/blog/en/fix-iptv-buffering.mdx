A stream that stalls every few seconds is the fastest way to give up on an
otherwise good setup. The good news: buffering is almost never the player's
fault, and it is almost always fixable. It comes from the *path* your video takes
— server, internet line, home network, device — and one weak link in that chain
is usually to blame.

Here is how to find it, from the most common cause to the least.

## Start with your own network

Most buffering is local. Before blaming anything else:

- **Get closer to the router, or use a cable.** Wi-Fi at the far end of the home
  is the single most common culprit. On a TV box, a wired connection ends the
  problem outright.
- **Check what else is using the line.** A large download or another 4K stream on
  the same connection starves your video of bandwidth.
- **Restart the router.** Unglamorous, genuinely effective.

## Then look at the stream itself

If the network is solid and one channel still struggles while others are fine,
the issue is upstream:

- **A single bad channel** usually means an overloaded server on the provider's
  side. Nothing on your end will fix it; try again later.
- **Everything struggles at peak hours** points to the provider being
  oversubscribed in the evening. That is a provider problem, not a player one.
- **A lower-bitrate version**, where your provider offers one, trades a little
  sharpness for a steady picture.

## Rule out the device

Older TV boxes and very long-running apps can run short of memory. A restart of
the app — or the box — clears it. Make sure the device is not mid-update while you
watch.

## What a good player can and cannot do

A player cannot manufacture bandwidth it does not have, but it can use what is
there well: buffering ahead smoothly, recovering from a dropped segment, and
never adding its own overhead by
[running ads or trackers in the background](/blog/no-ads-no-trackers/).

If you are still setting things up, it is worth confirming the basics first — that
[your playlist is added correctly](/blog/set-up-your-first-playlist/) and that
[the format your provider gave you](/blog/xtream-codes-vs-m3u/) matches how you
entered it. A surprising share of "buffering" turns out to be a small setup
detail, quickly put right.
