Replacing a phone is the moment a design decision you never had to think about
becomes visible. Most of your setup follows you without being asked. One part of
it — the part that matters most — follows you only if you still hold something
that can unlock it.

None of this is difficult, but the order of operations decides the outcome. Do
the migration while the old phone still works and is in your hands, and nothing
is lost. Leave it until afterwards and one piece may have to be rebuilt.

## What moves on its own

Favourites, watch progress and app settings sync in plain form, protected by
ordinary per-user access rules. Sign in on the new phone and they are simply
there: the channels you marked, the episode you were part-way through, the
preferences you set months ago and have long since forgotten.

They are stored unencrypted on purpose. Encrypting them would buy almost nothing,
because none of them are secrets, and it would leave a newly added device showing
you nothing useful until it had been unlocked.

## What arrives locked

Your provider credentials are treated as a different class of data, because they
*are* your subscription. The Xtream host, username and password, and any M3U
source URL, are encrypted on your device before they are stored at all. What
sits on a server is a label, a kind, and a block of ciphertext that nobody there
holds a key for. The reasoning and the mechanics are
[set out in full elsewhere](/blog/why-a-player-should-encrypt-your-credentials/).

So the new phone signs in and shows you something slightly strange: your
playlists are listed and marked **locked**. The name you gave a playlist is your
own words rather than a secret, so it was never encrypted and can be displayed.
The credential behind it cannot. The device knows the playlist exists. It cannot
yet connect with it.

## The two things that can unlock it

You need one of them.

**A device that is already unlocked.** The new phone generates an ephemeral key
pair and shows a QR code. The old phone, still signed in and still unlocked,
scans it and asks you to confirm. It then derives a shared secret with the new
device, seals the data key with that, and uploads the sealed result. The new
phone opens it with its own private key and stores what comes out in the iOS
Keychain or the Android Keystore. The key travels between two devices you own;
the server only ever carries a parcel it cannot open.

**Your sync password.** The data key is also wrapped with a key derived from that
password, and only the wrapped form is uploaded — the password itself never
crosses the network.

## The part that cannot be softened

If no unlocked device remains and the sync password is gone, the playlists are
gone with them. Not withheld — mathematically unavailable. There is nothing on
our side to reset, which is the entire point of the design and also the price of
it.

The recovery is undramatic. You re-add the playlist: find the credentials your
provider issued you and enter them again, which is
[the same few minutes it took the first time](/blog/set-up-your-first-playlist/).
Favourites, progress and settings are untouched, because they were never
encrypted. What you rebuild is one credential, not a setup.

## If sync was never turned on

Then there is nothing to migrate and nothing was ever exposed. An account exists
only to move your own data between your own devices, and the player works
completely without one —
[which is the model behaving as intended](/blog/bring-your-own-playlist/). It
does mean the new phone starts empty, and the playlist is added by hand exactly
as it was on the old one. Favourites are rebuilt too, since they only ever
existed on the device that recorded them.

## The order that works

Before the old phone leaves your possession:

1. Sign in on the new phone with the same account.
2. Pair the two while the old one is still signed in and unlocked.
3. Confirm that a playlist actually plays on the new phone — not merely that it
   appears in the list.

Then wipe the old one. If you have already wiped it and you still know your sync
password, you are fine. If neither is true, allow a minute to re-enter the
credentials and carry on; nothing else about your setup is affected, and your
subscription itself never moved at all.
