Sooner or later most households end up with more than one playlist. A second
provider for a language the first one lacks. A family member's subscription
alongside your own. Or the ordinary case: one provider who sends both an
Xtream login *and* an M3U link, and you would like to keep the second as a
spare. The wrong way to handle this is what a lot of players do — merge
everything into one enormous list. Here is why that is wrong, what "side by
side" should actually mean, and how it works.

## Why merging is the wrong answer

Two playlists are not one playlist with more lines. They come from different
servers, they carry different [guides](/blog/epg-explained/), they update on
different schedules, and each has its own login that can expire on its own
day. Pour them into one list and every one of those differences becomes a
symptom you cannot diagnose: the guide is right for half the channels; a
provider outage looks like random failures; the same channel appears twice
under two names and you never know which one is the one that stalls.

The alternative is simple: keep each source as a source. Vidonzo does exactly
that — you can add
[an Xtream login, an M3U link, or a local file](/blog/xtream-codes-vs-m3u/) as
many times as you have them, and switch between them from the playlist chip
at the top of the home screen. Nothing is merged. Everything about each one stays its own.

## What "its own" means in practice

**Its own guide.** An Xtream source brings its guide with the login. An M3U
source has an XMLTV address you set for *that* source. Two providers, two
guides, each matched to its own channels — so the identifiers never fight and
[an empty guide](/blog/epg-empty-or-wrong-times/) is always traceable to one
place.

**Its own resume points.** Continue watching is kept per source, per item. If
both providers carry the same series, the episode you were forty minutes into
on provider A is remembered as being on provider A. Switch sources and the
other list is where you left it, not where the first one was.

**Its own refresh.** A hosted playlist changes, and each source has its own
auto-refresh policy — every launch, hourly, six-hourly, daily, weekly, or
manual only — plus a *Refresh now* for the moment something looks missing. A
provider that reshuffles nightly can be re-read daily; the spare you rarely
touch can sit on manual. The cached list always shows instantly; the policy
only decides when it updates in the background.

**Its own credentials.** Two logins, held separately, and — since a login is
your subscription — each
[encrypted on the device](/blog/why-a-player-should-encrypt-your-credentials/) before
it goes anywhere near sync. One expiring does not touch the other.

## Switching, and the one thing that does cross over

Switching is a tap on that chip: the list, the guide, the
favourites and the resume points change together, because they all belong to
the source. It is deliberately not a filter over one merged pile; it is a
different library.

The one thing that *does* cross over is search. Global search runs across
live, films and series in the current source — and if you cannot find
something, switching sources and searching again takes a second. That is a
design choice: showing results from a source you are not currently in would
mean playing from a login you did not choose, and the confusion is worse than
the extra tap.

## Three arrangements this makes easy

**Login plus link from the same provider.** Add both. Use the Xtream login as
the everyday source — it is usually the richer one, with films and series
structured — and keep the M3U link as a spare for the day the login misbehaves.
Same channels, two doors.

**Two providers for two languages.** Each as its own source, each with its own
guide in its own language, each with favourites that make sense for the person
who watches it. Switching is the whole "which one" decision.

**A hosted playlist and a local copy.** [A URL stays current, a file is
frozen](/blog/playlist-url-vs-local-file/) — but a file needs no server. Keep
the link as the main source and a local file as the fallback for the evening
the server is unreachable. Two sources, one is always available.

## What to avoid

Do not add the same list twice hoping to organise it — favourites and
[categories](/blog/organize-large-playlists/) do that within a source, and a
duplicate just doubles the resume points to keep track of. And do not use
sources as a way of sharing one subscription with more people than it allows;
most providers limit simultaneous streams per login, and a second *source* on
the same login is still the same login.

The principle underneath is [the same one behind the whole player](/blog/bring-your-own-playlist/):
the playlist is yours, and a player's job is to treat each one you own with
the same respect — separately, and without ever pretending they were one
thing.
