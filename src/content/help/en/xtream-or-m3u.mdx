If your provider offers both, use **Xtream**. The short reason is metadata.

## What Xtream gives you

An Xtream Codes account is an API, not a file. Vidonzo can ask it for things a
flat playlist cannot express:

- live channels, films and series as **separate catalogues**
- series split into **seasons and episodes**
- posters, ratings, synopses and cast
- the programme guide, without a second URL
- category structure as the provider defined it

## What M3U gives you

An M3U file is a list of stream URLs with a name and sometimes a logo. That is
all it can carry. Vidonzo will group by the `group-title` attribute where one
exists, but films and episodes arrive as a flat list of names, because that is
what the format contains.

M3U is still the right choice when:

- your provider only offers M3U
- you keep a hand-made list of a few dozen channels
- you want a playlist that works with **no server at all**, from a local file

## Adding a guide to an M3U playlist

If your provider publishes an XMLTV guide separately, add its URL in
**Settings → Programme guide**. Live channels then show what is on now and next,
with the time zone handled correctly.

## Using both

You can keep several sources side by side — an Xtream account for the main
catalogue and a local M3U for a handful of channels you added yourself — and
switch between them from the source picker.
